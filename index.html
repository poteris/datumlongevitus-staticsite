Children die because data isn’t shared.

Surely the data that could protect a child is already joined up? It is not. A family can be known to the school, the council and the police, and still no one sees the whole picture until it is too late. In Bristol, the Think Family Database changed that, bringing together around 35 public sector datasets so that services can reach families before a crisis rather than after one. That is one city. What about every other child?

Proof 02 / 03

Lives can’t be saved if data is lost.

When studies about vulnerable children ended, their data was locked away. No one had ever put it somewhere the next researcher could use it. Then James brought the first non-governmental research dataset into the Office for National Statistics. The first. Ever. How many children could have been helped by everything that was lost before? It cannot be the last.

Proof 03 / 03

Can DATA reduce violence?

It sounds absurd. A national study, Boxing to End Youth Violence, is finding out whether boxing can keep young people in low-income areas away from violence. It depended on every partner trusting that the young people taking part would be protected. Datum brought them all to consensus. If the answer is yes, it changes how young people are kept safe in every town that needs it. Compliance reduces violence.

Each of these happened because someone got the governance right. Far more often, no one does, and data that could save a life stays exactly where it is. Datum Longevitus is built to change that.

We are the

THe DOTS.

Protect, share and unlock: from compliance for a single charity to the secure environments that let whole consortia work with data. Most of the data world wants to be a dot: the platform, the technology, the company that holds the data. We would rather be the line that connects the dots.

We design and manage the line using a combination of compliance and the worlds most advanced technologies: Signal pushing; Memory infrastructures; Secure 5 Safes Environments; Federated Research & Learning Environments; Human1; Fractal; Data Sapians; Prolinx; MetaData Blockchains... and good old fashioned AWS, Microsoft and Google compliance.


Data is shared constantly. Every video call, every email, every record that passes between a charity and a local authority travels through infrastructure most people never think about. What makes that sharing trustworthy is governance: knowing where the data is coming from, where it is going, who is using it, why they are using it, and whether they are allowed to. When those questions cannot be answered, trust is lost and the data stays locked away. Answering them properly is what allows us to be put to good use.

We protect it.

A charity that cannot prove it protects people’s data will struggle to win the funding it needs to help them. We work with charities and research organisations by choice, because they use data for public benefit. For them, we act as an outsourced Data Protection Officer: carrying out a gap analysis, then maintaining the policies, registers, privacy notices, DPIAs, data sharing agreements and rights requests that sit underneath every project. On research consortia, we often lead data protection for the whole group, even when we are not the lead partner.

The Name

DatumOne piece of information
+
LongevitusFrom longevity
=
Information that lasts

A datum is one piece of information. Longevitus comes from longevity. Put them together and you have the whole idea: information that lasts.

Information being passed on is how cells remember and how people learn. It is how one generation hands what it knows to the next, and how life got from single cells to travelling to the stars. When it stops being handed on, progress stops with it.

Research data is no different. Most of it is used once and filed away, and whatever it could have taught the next team goes with it. We want it kept, protected and passed on, so that it goes on helping people long after the original study has ended.

What we learn, and what we leave behind, should outlast us.
